Section A · Experimental Probability⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • P(E) = number of trials in which E happenedtotal number of trials.
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1, and P(not E) = 1 − P(E).
WORKED EXAMPLE
A coin is tossed 500 times and heads comes 285 times. Find P(head) and P(tail).
→ P(H) = 285500 = 0.57.
→ P(T) = 1 − 0.57 = 0.43.
  1. A1A die is thrown 300 times and 6 comes 48 times. Find P(6) and P(not 6).
  2. A2A coin is tossed 80 times and tails comes 36 times. Find P(head).
  3. A3Can an experimental probability be 1.2? Why?
  4. A4If P(E) = 0.35, find P(not E).
Section B · Probability from Tables⏱ 30 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Read the frequency of the event from the table and divide by the total.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Of 40 students, 14 have blood group O. Find P(O).
→ 1440 = 0.35.
  1. B1400 families with 2 children: 0 girls – 90, 1 girl – 210, 2 girls – 100. Find P(exactly 1 girl).
  2. B2In the same table, find P(2 girls) and P(at least one girl).
  3. B3Blood groups of 40 students: O 14, A 10, B 12, AB 4. Find P(AB).
  4. B4Eleven 5 kg flour bags weigh (kg): 4.97, 5.05, 5.08, 5.03, 5.00, 5.06, 5.08, 4.98, 5.04, 5.07, 5.00. Find P(a bag has more than 5 kg).
Section C · Tossing Coins⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Use the frequency of each outcome. 'At least' means add the frequencies of all outcomes that fit.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Two coins are tossed 500 times: 2 heads 105, 1 head 275, 0 heads 120. Find P(1 head).
→ 275500 = 0.55.
  1. C1In the same experiment, find P(no head).
  2. C2Three coins are tossed 200 times: 3 heads 23, 2 heads 72, 1 head 77, 0 heads 28. Find P(2 heads).
  3. C3In the same experiment, find P(at least 2 heads).
  4. C4In the same experiment, find P(no head).
Section D · Real Life⏱ 20 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Expected number = probability × total.
WORKED EXAMPLE
15 of 250 bulbs tested were defective. Find P(defective).
→ 15250 = 0.06.
  1. D1How many defective bulbs would you expect in a box of 1,000?
  2. D2A forecast was correct on 292 of 365 days. Find P(correct forecast).
  3. D3A batsman hit 6 boundaries in 30 balls. Find P(he does NOT hit a boundary).
Section E · Think About It⏱ 20 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Experimental probability depends on the trials done.
WORKED EXAMPLE
A coin gave 5 heads in 10 tosses. Is the next toss sure to be a tail?
→ No – each toss is independent.
→ Experimental results only estimate probability.
  1. E1If an event never happened in 100 trials, what is its experimental probability?
  2. E2What is the sum of the probabilities of all the outcomes of an experiment?
  3. E3Why do more trials give a better estimate of probability?
