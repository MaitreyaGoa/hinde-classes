Section A · Lines of Symmetry⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • A line of symmetry divides a figure into two halves that match exactly when folded (mirror halves).
  • Fold, or imagine a mirror on the line: does each half reflect onto the other?
WORKED EXAMPLE
How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle (not a square) have?
→ The lines through the midpoints of opposite sides: 2.
→ The diagonals are NOT lines of symmetry.
  1. A1Square
  2. A2Equilateral triangle
  3. A3Isosceles triangle (not equilateral)
  4. A4Scalene triangle
  5. A5Circle
  6. A6Regular hexagon
  7. A7Parallelogram that is not a rectangle
  8. A8Kite

Section C · Making Symmetric Figures⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Each point and its image are the same distance from the mirror line, on opposite sides.
  • Use squared paper: count boxes from the mirror line.
WORKED EXAMPLE
A dot is 3 boxes to the left of a vertical mirror line. Where is its image?
→ 3 boxes to the right of the line, in the same row.
  1. C1A dot is 5 boxes above a horizontal mirror line. Where is its image?
  2. C2On squared paper, draw a shape with exactly one line of symmetry and mark the line.
  3. C3Draw a shape with exactly two lines of symmetry and mark them.
  4. C4Draw half of a house shape next to a vertical line and complete it so the line is a line of symmetry.
Section D · Rotational Symmetry⏱ 30 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • A figure has rotational symmetry if it looks the same after turning it less than a full turn about its centre.
  • Order = number of times it looks the same in one full turn. Smallest angle = 360° ÷ order.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the order and smallest angle of rotational symmetry of a square.
→ It looks the same after 90°, 180°, 270°, 360°.
→ Order 4, angle 360° ÷ 4 = 90°.
  1. D1Equilateral triangle: order and angle?
  2. D2Rectangle: order and angle?
  3. D3Regular hexagon: order and angle?
  4. D4Regular pentagon: order and angle?
  5. D5Letter S: order and angle?
  6. D6Name a figure that has a line of symmetry but no rotational symmetry.
Section E · Mixed Questions⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Use: angle × order = 360°.
  • A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry and order n.
WORKED EXAMPLE
A regular polygon has 8 sides. Find its lines of symmetry and its angle of rotation.
→ 8 lines; order 8; 360° ÷ 8 = 45°.
  1. E1A figure looks the same after every 40° turn. What is its order?
  2. E2Can a figure have rotational symmetry but no line of symmetry? Give an example.
  3. E3A regular polygon has 10 lines of symmetry. Name it and find its angle of rotation.
  4. E4How many lines of symmetry and what order does the letter H have?
