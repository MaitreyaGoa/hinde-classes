Section A · Fraction × Whole Number⏱ 15 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Multiply the whole number with the numerator only. The denominator stays the same.
  • Cancel common factors first to keep numbers small.
  • For a mixed number, change it to an improper fraction first.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find 6 × 58.
→ 6 × 58 = 308.
→ Simplify: 308 = 154 = 334.
  1. A13 × 27
  2. A25 × 310
  3. A34 × 123
  4. A429 × 6
  5. A57 × 314
Section B · Fraction of a Quantity⏱ 15 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • 'of' means multiply.
  • Divide the quantity by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator.
  • Always write the unit (kg, ₹, minutes) in the answer.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find 34 of 60 kg.
→ 60 ÷ 4 = 15.
→ 15 × 3 = 45.
→ Answer: 45 kg.
  1. B114 of 36
  2. B235 of 40
  3. B323 of 45 kg
  4. B458 of ₹480
  5. B534 of 2 hours (answer in minutes)
Section C · Fraction × Fraction⏱ 20 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Multiply numerator × numerator and denominator × denominator.
  • Cancel common factors (top with bottom) BEFORE multiplying.
  • Change mixed numbers to improper fractions first.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find 49 × 38.
→ Cancel 4 with 8 → 1 and 2; cancel 3 with 9 → 1 and 3.
→ 13 × 12 = 16.
  1. C123 × 45
  2. C238 × 49
  3. C356 × 310
  4. C4112 × 223
  5. C5214 × 113
  6. C6712 × 635
Section D · Reciprocal and Division of Fractions⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • The reciprocal of ab is ba. (Reciprocal of 5 is 15.)
  • To divide by a fraction, MULTIPLY by its reciprocal: ab ÷ cd = ab × dc.
  • Change mixed numbers to improper fractions first, then cancel and multiply.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find 35 ÷ 910.
→ Reciprocal of 910 is 109.
→ 35 × 109: cancel 3 with 9 and 5 with 10 → 11 × 23.
→ Answer: 23.
  1. D1Write the reciprocals of 59, 7 and 16.
  2. D26 ÷ 23
  3. D334 ÷ 3
  4. D445 ÷ 215
  5. D5212 ÷ 114
  6. D656 ÷ 512
Section E · Word Problems⏱ 35 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Read carefully. Decide whether you need to multiply ('of', 'each', 'area') or divide ('how many pieces', 'shared equally').
  • Write the number sentence first, then solve step by step.
  • Write the final answer with its unit.
WORKED EXAMPLE
A jug holds 34 litre. How much do 8 such jugs hold?
→ Each × number → multiply: 8 × 34 = 244 = 6.
→ 8 jugs hold 6 litres.

Section F · Extra Practice⏱ 20 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • These are slightly harder. Do BODMAS: brackets first, then ÷ and ×, then + and −.
  • Take your time and check each step.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find (12 × 45) ÷ 25.
→ Bracket: 12 × 45 = 25.
→ 25 ÷ 25 = 1.
