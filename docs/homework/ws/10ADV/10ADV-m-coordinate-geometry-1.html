Section A · Distance Formula – Higher Order⏱ 40 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Square both sides when comparing distances to avoid roots.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the relation between x and y so that (x, y) is equidistant from (3, 6) and (−3, 4).
→ (x − 3)² + (y − 6)² = (x + 3)² + (y − 4)².
→ −12x − 4y + 20 = 0 → 3x + y = 5.
  1. A1Find the point on the x-axis equidistant from (7, 6) and (3, 4).
  2. A2Q(0, 1) is equidistant from P(5, −3) and R(x, 6). Find x, QR and PR.
  3. A3Show that (−1, −2), (1, 0), (−1, 2), (−3, 0) form a square.
  4. A4Show that (a, a), (−a, −a), (−√3a, √3a) form an equilateral triangle.
  5. A5Is the triangle (−2, 0), (2, 0), (0, 2) right-angled? Justify.
Section B · Section Formula – Higher Order⏱ 45 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • For an unknown ratio use k : 1. A point on the x-axis has y = 0; on the y-axis x = 0.
WORKED EXAMPLE
In what ratio does the y-axis divide the join of (5, −6) and (−1, −4)? Find the point.
→ (−k + 5k + 1, …) has x = 0 → k = 5.
→ 5 : 1; point (0, −133).
  1. B1Find the points that divide the join of (−2, 2) and (2, 8) into four equal parts.
  2. B2(−2, −1), (1, 0), (x, 3), (1, y) are the vertices of a parallelogram in order. Find x and y.
  3. B3A(−2, −2), B(2, −4). P is on AB with AP = 37AB. Find P.
  4. B4Find the ratio in which (−1, 6) divides the join of (−3, 10) and (6, −8).
  5. B5Find the points of trisection of the join of (−3, −5) and (−6, −8).
Section C · Board Challenge⏱ 35 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Expand carefully; collect terms.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the length of the median AD of △ABC with A(−1, 3), B(1, −1), C(5, 1).
→ D = mid-point of BC = (3, 0).
→ AD = √(16 + 9) = 5.
  1. C1P(x, y) is equidistant from A(a + b, b − a) and B(a − b, a + b). Prove bx = ay.
  2. C2The mid-point of the join of (a, −1) and (5, b) is (3, 2). Find a and b.
  3. C3If A(4, 3), B(−1, y) and C(3, 4) form a right triangle with right angle at A, find y.
