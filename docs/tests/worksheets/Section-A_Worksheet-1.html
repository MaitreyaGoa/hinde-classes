Chapter 1

Real Numbers

Exam weight: 4 marks (2 VSA + 1 SA-I) | Dropped: Euclid's Division Lemma; Revisiting Rational Numbers & Decimal Expansions

Concept Notes

  • Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: every composite number factorises uniquely into primes.
  • HCF = product of smallest powers of common primes. LCM = product of greatest powers of all primes.
  • Key relation (two numbers only): HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.
  • Proving irrationality: assume rational → contradiction, using the known irrationality of √2, √3, √5, etc.

VSA (1 mark) — 12 Qs

  1. Express 140 as a product of its prime factors.
  2. Express 468 as a product of its prime factors.
  3. Find the HCF of 96 and 404 by prime factorisation.
  4. Find the LCM of 96 and 404 by prime factorisation.
  5. If the product of two numbers is 1800 and their HCF is 15, find their LCM.
  6. The LCM of two numbers is 180 and their HCF is 6. If one number is 30, find the other.
  7. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their LCM is 240. Find their HCF.
  8. If one number is 2³×3²×5 and another is 2²×3³×5², find their HCF.
  9. Using the same two numbers as Q8, find their LCM.
  10. The HCF and LCM of two numbers are 9 and 459. If one number is 27, find the other.
  11. Express 3825 as a product of its prime factors.
  12. Express 7429 as a product of its prime factors.

  2. Two tankers contain 850 L and 680 L of petrol. Find the maximum capacity of a container that can measure the petrol of either tanker an exact number of times.
  3. There are 32 students in Section A and 36 in Section B of Class X. Find the minimum number of books needed so they can be distributed equally among either section.
  4. Three bells ring at intervals of 12, 15, 18 minutes. If they ring together at 8:00 a.m., after how many minutes will they next ring together?
  5. Prove that 5 − √3 is an irrational number.
  6. Prove that 2 + 3√5 is an irrational number.
  7. A courtyard is 15 m 17 cm long and 9 m 2 cm broad. Find the minimum number of square tiles required to pave it (maximum possible tile side).
  8. Three containers contain 403 L, 434 L, and 465 L of a mixture. Find the maximum capacity of a container that can measure the difference between any two quantities exactly.

Chapter 2

Polynomials

Exam weight: 4 marks (2 VSA + 1 SA-I) | Dropped: Division Algorithm for Polynomials

Concept Notes

  • For quadratic ax²+bx+c: Sum of zeros = −b/a, Product of zeros = c/a.
  • To form a polynomial from given zeros α, β: x² − (α+β)x + αβ (scale up to clear fractions).
  • Always verify the relationship after finding zeros by factorisation.

  1. Form a quadratic polynomial whose zeros are 4 and −1.

  3. If the sum of zeros of a quadratic polynomial is 5 and the product is 6, form the polynomial.
  4. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeros are −3 and 1/2.
  5. If α, β are zeros of a quadratic polynomial such that α+β = 7, αβ = 12, form the polynomial.
  6. The sum and product of the zeros of a quadratic polynomial are 0 and −9 respectively. Find the polynomial.
  7. If one zero of the polynomial x² − 5x + k is 2, find k.
  8. If the zeros of x² + (a+1)x + b are 2 and −3, find a and b.
  9. If the product of zeros of 4x² − kx − 9 is 3 times the sum of zeros, find k.
  10. If the sum of zeros of kx² + 3x − 5 is 3/5, find k.

Chapter 3

Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

Exam weight: 9 marks (1 VSA + 1 Assertion-Reason + 1 SA-II + 1 LA) | Dropped: Cross-Multiplication Method; Equations Reducible to a Pair of Linear Equations

Concept Notes

  • Consistency: unique solution if a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂; infinite solutions if a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂; no solution if a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂.
  • Elimination method: make one variable's coefficients equal (in magnitude) and add/subtract.
  • Substitution method: express one variable in terms of the other, substitute into the second equation.
  • Word problems: define variables clearly, form two equations, solve, and verify back in context.

VSA (1 mark) — 8 Qs

  1. Solve: x+y=7, x−y=3. Find x and y.
  2. If 2x+3y=12 and x=3, find y.
  3. For what value of k do kx+2y=5 and 3x+y=1 represent parallel lines?
  4. Find k for which 2x+3y=5, 4x+6y=k has infinitely many solutions.
  5. If x=2, y=1 is a solution of 2x+3y=k, find k.
  6. State whether x+2y=5, 2x+4y=10 has a unique, infinite, or no solution.
  7. If 3x−y=5 and x+y=3, find the value of x.
  8. Find k so that 3x+2y=4, 9x+6y=k has no solution.

Assertion–Reason (1 mark) — 5 Qs  (select: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R doesn't explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true)

  1. A: The pair 2x+3y=7, 4x+6y=14 has infinitely many solutions. R: A pair has infinitely many solutions if a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂.
  2. A: The pair x+y=5, 2x+2y=8 has no solution. R: No solution exists if a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂.
  3. A: Lines 3x−4y+5=0 and 6x−8y+7=0 are parallel. R: Parallel lines satisfy a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂.
  4. A: The pair x−2y=0, 3x+4y−20=0 has a unique solution. R: A unique solution exists if a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂.
  5. A: The equations 2x+3y=9 and 4x+6y=18 are inconsistent. R: They have infinitely many solutions.

Chapter 4

Quadratic Equations

Exam weight: 8 marks (1 VSA + 1 SA-II + 1 LA) | Dropped: Completing the Square method

Concept Notes

  • Discriminant: D = b²−4ac. Real & distinct roots if D>0; equal if D=0; not real if D<0.
  • Factorisation: split the middle term so the two parts multiply to ac and add to b.
  • Quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a
  • Word problems: translate into a quadratic equation, solve, and reject any root that doesn't fit context (negative length/age, etc.).

VSA (1 mark) — 8 Qs

  1. Find the value(s) of k for which the discriminant of 2x²+kx+3=0 is 25.
  2. Find k for which x²−4x+k=0 has equal roots.
  3. One root of x²−6x+k=0 is 4. Find k.
  4. If 3 is a root of 2x²+px−15=0, find p.
  5. Find k for which x=2 is a root of kx²−3x+2=0.
  6. Show that 3x²−4x+4/3=0 has equal roots (find the discriminant).
  7. Find the discriminant of 5x²−6x−2=0.
  8. Find the value(s) of k for which x²+kx+64=0 has equal roots.

Chapter 5

Arithmetic Progressions

Exam weight: 5 marks (2 VSA + 1 SA-II) | No deletions

Concept Notes

  • nth term: an = a + (n−1)d
  • Sum of n terms: Sn = n/2 [2a+(n−1)d] or n/2 (a+an)
  • nth term from the end (last term l): l − (n−1)d
  • Word problems: identify a (first term) and d (common difference) from the situation, then apply the right formula.

VSA (1 mark) — 10 Qs

  1. Find the 15th term of the AP: 7, 13, 19, …
  2. Which term of the AP 21, 18, 15, … is −81?
  3. Find the common difference if the first term is 5 and the 20th term is 62.
  4. The 10th term of an AP is 52 and the 15th term is 77. Find the first term and common difference.
  5. Find the 25th term from the end of the AP: 3, 8, 13, …, 253.
  6. If the sum of the 3rd and 8th terms of an AP is 7, and the sum of the 7th and 14th terms is −3, find the common difference.
  7. Find the 10th term of the AP: −5, −1, 3, 7, …
  8. If a=3, d=−2 for an AP, find the 12th term.
  9. In the AP 3, 15, 27, 39, …, which term is 120 more than its 21st term?
  10. The common difference of an AP is 5 and its 18th term is 3. Find its 20th term.

  5. Find three numbers in AP whose sum is 21 and product is 231.
