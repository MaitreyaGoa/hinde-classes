Section A The Notion of Letter-Numbers

Concept Notes
  • A letter such as a, s or n used to stand for a number is called a letter-number. An expression containing letter-numbers, like a + 3, is called an algebraic expression.
  • To build an expression, first describe the relationship in words ('Shabnam's age = Aftab's age + 3'), then replace the phrase by a letter: s = a + 3.
  • An expression is a formula: replacing the letter-number by any value gives the answer for that case. If a = 23 in a + 3, the value is 26.
  • One situation can need two or more letter-numbers, e.g. cost of c coconuts at ₹35 and j kg jaggery at ₹60 is c × 35 + j × 60.

Section B Revisiting Arithmetic Expressions

Concept Notes
  • An expression can be seen as a sum of terms; a minus sign belongs to the term after it: 23 – 10 × 2 = 23 + (-10 × 2).
  • Swapping (changing the order of terms) and grouping (bracketing conveniently) never change the value of an expression.
  • A bracket with a minus sign outside can be removed by changing the sign of every term inside: 68 – (18 + 13) = 68 – 18 – 13.
  • Multiplication is done before adding terms: in 7 × 4 + 9 × 6, evaluate 28 and 54 first, then add.

Section C Omission of the Multiplication Symbol & Substitution

Concept Notes
  • As a standard practice, 4 × n is shortened to 4n – the number is written first, then the letter. So 5m + 3 means 5 × m + 3.
  • To evaluate an expression, replace every letter-number by its value and follow the usual rules: 7k at k = 4 is 7 × 4 = 28.
  • Substituting negative values needs care with signs: 10 – a at a = -4 is 10 – (-4) = 14.
  • The nth term of a sequence can often be written as an expression: for 4, 8, 12, 16, ... the nth term is 4n.

Section D Simplification of Algebraic Expressions

Concept Notes
  • Terms with the same letter-number, like 5c, 3c, 10c, are called like terms; terms with different letter-numbers, like 18c and 11d, are unlike terms.
  • Like terms can be combined into one term using the distributive property: 5c + 3c + 10c = (5 + 3 + 10)c = 18c. Unlike terms cannot be combined.
  • Two expressions are equal if they take the same value for every replacement of the letter-numbers: l + b + l + b and 2l + 2b both give 14 at l = 3, b = 4.
  • Brackets are opened just as in arithmetic; a minus before a bracket flips the sign of every term inside: (40x + 75y) – (6x + 10y) = 40x + 75y – 6x – 10y.

Section E Adding, Subtracting & Mending Mistakes

Concept Notes
  • To add expressions, remove brackets and combine like terms; to subtract, change the sign of every term of the expression being subtracted, then combine.
  • 'Subtract A from B' means B – A – the order matters!
  • An expression is in its simplest form when brackets are removed, all like terms are combined, and plain numbers are added. Unlike terms must be left as they are: 3a + 2b is already simplest – it is NOT 5.
  • In a correct simplest form, the number of terms is one more than the number of different letter-numbers at most (each letter's terms merge into one, plus possibly one number term).

Section F Pick Patterns & Reveal Relationships

Concept Notes
  • A formula found from a few cases can be written as an expression, e.g. a number machine doing 'twice the first input minus the second' is 2a – b.
  • Repeating designs of period 3 sit at positions 3n, 3n – 1 and 3n – 2; the remainder on dividing a position by 3 tells which design is there (remainder 0 -> multiple of 3).
  • In an endless calendar, the cell right of a is a + 1, below is a + 7, diagonal is a + 8. Algebra proves facts for ALL squares at once – e.g. both diagonal sums equal 2a + 8.
  • Matchstick-type patterns growing by a fixed amount have expressions like 3 + 2(y – 1), which simplifies to 2y + 1.
