Section A · 1-Mark Questions (VSA)⏱ 20 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Write the prime factorisation as a product of powers, smallest prime first.
  • For any two positive integers a and b: HCF × LCM = a × b.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Express 140 as a product of its prime factors.
→ 140 = 2 × 70 = 2 × 2 × 35 = 2² × 5 × 7.
  1. A1Express 156 as a product of its prime factors.
  2. A2HCF of two numbers is 9 and their product is 1620. Find their LCM.
  3. A3If HCF(26, 91) = 13, find LCM(26, 91).
  4. A4Find the LCM of 2³ × 3 × 5 and 2² × 3³.
  5. A5Can a number of the form 6ⁿ end with the digit 0? Give a reason.
  6. A6Find the HCF of 96 and 404 by prime factorisation.
Section B · HCF and LCM by Prime Factorisation⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • HCF = product of the SMALLEST power of each COMMON prime.
  • LCM = product of the GREATEST power of EVERY prime that appears.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the HCF and LCM of 12, 15 and 21.
→ 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5, 21 = 3 × 7.
→ HCF = 3, LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 × 7 = 420.
  1. B1Find the LCM of 96 and 404, and check that HCF × LCM = product.
  2. B2Find the HCF and LCM of 17, 23 and 29.
  3. B3Find the HCF and LCM of 8, 9 and 25.
  4. B4Find the HCF and LCM of 336 and 54.
  5. B5Explain why 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is a composite number.
Section C · Irrational Numbers – Proofs (3 marks)⏱ 25 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Assume the opposite (that the number is rational, = ab in lowest terms).
  • Square, and show that a prime divides both a and b – a contradiction.
  • For 3 + 2√5: if it were rational, √5 = rational − 32 would be rational – contradiction.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Prove that √5 is irrational.
→ Let √5 = ab, a and b co-prime. Then a² = 5b², so 5 divides a; write a = 5c.
→ 25c² = 5b² → b² = 5c², so 5 divides b too – contradiction. Hence √5 is irrational.
  1. C1Prove that √3 is irrational.
  2. C2Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational.
  3. C3Prove that 5 − √3 is irrational.
  4. C4Prove that 1√2 is irrational.
Section D · Word Problems (2 and 3 marks)⏱ 30 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • 'Together again / at the same time / least number' → LCM.
  • 'Greatest / largest / maximum equal groups' → HCF.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Three bells ring every 6, 8 and 12 minutes. They ring together at 8 am. When do they next ring together?
→ LCM(6, 8, 12) = 24.
→ At 8:24 am.
  1. D1Sonia takes 18 minutes and Ravi 12 minutes to go round a field. They start together. After how many minutes will they meet again at the start?
  2. D2An army contingent of 616 members marches behind a band of 32 members in the same number of columns. What is the maximum number of columns?
  3. D3Find the minimum number of sweets that can be shared equally among 12, 15 or 20 children with none left over.
  4. D4Find the side of the largest square tile that can exactly cover a floor 6 m 24 cm by 4 m 32 cm.
  5. D5Find the smallest number which leaves remainder 3 when divided by 6, 8 or 10.
Section E · Assertion – Reason⏱ 20 min
HOW TO SOLVE
  • Choose: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.
WORKED EXAMPLE
A: HCF(33, 44) × LCM(33, 44) = 33 × 44. R: HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers for any two positive integers.
→ Both are true and R explains A → (a).
  1. E1A: √2 + √3 is irrational. R: The sum of two irrational numbers is always irrational.
  2. E2A: 2 × 3 × 5 + 5 is composite. R: A number with a factor other than 1 and itself is composite.
  3. E3A: The HCF of two consecutive natural numbers is 1. R: Two consecutive natural numbers are always co-prime.
